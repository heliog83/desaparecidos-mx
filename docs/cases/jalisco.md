## What Confidential Records Hide Here

- status split for confidential records
- date of disappearance
- date of report/registration
- sex
- birth date / age
- municipality

Interpretation rule: confidential records still count as unresolved people, but they
cannot be used for year, sex, age, municipality, or status-specific analysis.

## Evidence Checklist

### Local Search/Fiscalia Rules About Publication

Question: does the state have rules, protocols, notices, or registry language explaining
what gets published, withheld, anonymized, or treated as confidential?

Status: VERIFIED (2026-07-06). Key finding — Jalisco runs a **parallel state registry
apparatus** distinct from the federal RNPDNO:

- State law: Ley de Personas Desaparecidas del Estado de Jalisco, approved Feb 2021
  after years of pressure from families ("Día histórico", ZonaDocs 2021-02-25,
  https://www.zonadocs.mx/2021/02/25/dia-historico-celebran-familias-aprobacion-de-ley-de-personas-desaparecida-de-jalisco/).
  Collectives had demanded participation since 2019 ("¡Sin las familias no!").
- State registry: REPD with its own public version
  (https://version-publica-repd.jalisco.gob.mx/) plus SISOVID
  (https://sisovid.jalisco.gob.mx/), run by the state, with different cut-off dates and
  totals than RNPDNO.
- Fiscalía Especial en Personas Desaparecidas maintains its own portal incl. clandestine
  grave (fosas) information (https://fiscaliaenpersonasdesaparecidas.jalisco.gob.mx/).
- **The blanket-confidentiality rule (the smoking gun):** in the CNB's own consulta to
  INAI (Oficio SEGOB/CNBP/1349/2020, p. 11 n. 3 — local copy in
  `data/external/legal_docs/`), the CNB records that "la Fiscalía de Jalisco ... ha
  manifestado que la información que remite al RNPDNO no puede ser pública en virtud de
  que las familias ... han expresado directamente no dar su consentimiento", i.e. the
  Fiscalía declares its ENTIRE federal submission non-publishable. This is the earliest
  documented institutional explanation for Jalisco's outlier confidential share, and it
  is a state-fiscalía practice layered on the federal consent rule (see
  [../opacity-mechanism.md](../opacity-mechanism.md)).

### Official Statements About Privacy/Redaction

Question: has the state justified confidentiality as privacy, victim protection, child
protection, investigation integrity, or another reason?

Status: VERIFIED (2026-07-06).

- Justification on record (2020): family non-consent, asserted wholesale by the Fiscalía
  (oficio footnote above). No evidence the consent declarations exist per case; the CNB
  itself could not produce them when INAI ordered database release (RRA 09082/20).
- "Jalisco no manipula su registro, ni borra cifras de personas desaparecidas" — official
  government statement, Jan 2024 (https://www.jalisco.gob.mx/es/prensa/noticias/160564;
  page under maintenance when consulted 2026-07-06; coverage: Proceso 2024-01-23,
  https://www.proceso.com.mx/nacional/2024/1/23/el-gobierno-de-jalisco-niega-que-manipule-las-cifras-de-personas-desaparecidas-322654.html).
  Denial issued in response to accusations of hiding/erasing records (below).
- Gov. Pablo Lemus administration (2025): denies records were deleted from the national
  registry, promises daily/real-time REPD updates instead of monthly (Milenio,
  https://www.milenio.com/politica/comunidad/actualizaran-registro-de-desaparecidos-en-jalisco-en-tiempo-real).
- Pattern: official statements defend the *state* registry's integrity; none consulted so
  far explains why records Jalisco sends to the *federal* registry are confidentialized.

### Collective Or Journalist Criticism

Question: have families, collectives, journalists, NGOs, or transparency advocates
criticized registry opacity, undercounting, reclassification, or deleted records in this state?

Status: VERIFIED (2026-07-06). Abundant, sustained, specific:

- **Stopped feeding the federal registry:** from 2022 under Gov. Alfaro, Jalisco stopped
  reporting disappearances to the RNPDNO, counting them only in the state REPD; critics
  put the resulting gap at up to ~2,986 people (Animal Político,
  https://animalpolitico.com/verificacion-de-hechos/te-explico/jalisco-no-reporta-todas-las-desapariciones-en-registro-estatal;
  El Informador 2023-05-09, "Borran cifras de Jalisco en el registro nacional",
  https://www.informador.mx/Desaparecidos-Borran-cifras-de-Jalisco-en-el-registro-nacional-l202305090001.html).
- **ZonaDocs "El engaño estadístico" (2024-11-21):** Jalisco is the state with the largest
  under-registration found (2,137 disappeared not registered, 2019–jun 2024) AND 1,026
  cases with active search notices that nonetheless appear CONFIDENCIAL in the RNPDNO.
  Case on record: María de Jesús Solís (son disappeared Zapopan 2020-09-18) never signed
  any confidentiality request and wants maximum visibility
  (https://www.zonadocs.mx/2024/11/21/desaparecidos-el-engano-estadistico-de-mexico/).
- **CEPAD:** "Fiscalía Especializada ... ineficaz e ineficiente" (ZonaDocs 2022-10-19) —
  78% of surveyed searching families say the Fiscalía is remiss; 47% rate treatment bad
  or very bad. Anna Karolina Chimiak (CEPAD co-director): practices seek to "prevent
  cases from becoming public, keep them in impunity." CEPAD also runs a monitoring
  micro-site (https://www.cepad.org.mx/infodh/desaparicion-de-personas/).
- **Red Lupa (ZonaDocs 2025-10-08):** "En Jalisco, la desaparición no es un fenómeno
  colateral, sino central" — 14,982 disappeared at 2025-05-16; disputes the implausible
  drop to 160 new cases reported in 2024, attributing it to "subregistro, dilaciones o
  fallas en el sistema de denuncia oficial"; flags disconnect between the state search
  commission and the Fiscalía
  (https://www.zonadocs.mx/2025/10/08/en-jalisco-la-desaparicion-no-es-un-fenomeno-colateral-sino-central-red-lupa/).
- Families also denounced search-notice removals ahead of the 2026 World Cup (Conecta
  Arizona, https://conectaarizona.com/crisis-desaparecidos-jalisco-retiro-fichas-mundial-2026/;
  single source — corroborate before citing).

### Fosas / Collective Search Context

Question: has the state had major clandestine-grave discoveries or search-collective
actions that could help explain why opacity matters locally?

Status: VERIFIED (2026-07-06).

- **Teuchitlán / Rancho Izaguirre (2025-03-05):** Guerreros Buscadores de Jalisco found a
  CJNG recruitment-and-extermination site — clothing, shoes, backpacks, notebooks, bone
  fragments. The National Guard had already raided the ranch in Sept 2024 (10 detained);
  the collective documented the remains six months later; national scandal about what state
  records failed to document. Candidate Stage-2 anchor event.
- "Jalisco vive un estado de emergencia forense" — Por Amor A Ellxs collective (ZonaDocs
  2023-09-21).
- **Lethal risk to searchers (2025):** Teresa González Murillo attacked and died;
  María del Carmen Morales (Guerreros Buscadores) murdered along with her son while
  searching for her other disappeared son (Red Lupa/ZonaDocs above).

## Preliminary Case Question

What would explain why Jalisco has this combination of crisis scale and
public-registry opacity?

**Working answer (2026-07-06):** a documented chain of state-institutional practice, not
family preference: (1) since at least 2020, the Fiscalía de Jalisco blanket-declares its
RNPDNO submissions non-publishable, invoking family non-consent it has not evidenced
per-case (CNB oficio to INAI, footnote 3); (2) from 2022 the state stopped feeding the
federal registry, counting cases only in its parallel REPD; (3) journalists documented
1,026 Jalisco cases with public search notices that are nonetheless CONFIDENCIAL, and at
least one mother on record who never requested confidentiality. Jalisco's 67%
confidential share is therefore best described as fiscalía publication policy plus
federal-state registry conflict.
