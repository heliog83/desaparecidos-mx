## Phase 0 — Repository discovery

**Canonical article.** `reports/article.html` is generated by `src/build_page.py`
from `reports/article_draft.md`. The build also pulls in `src/article_interactive.{js,css}`,
the PNGs in `reports/figures/article/`, and the derived tables in
`data/publication_freezes/2026-08-15/derived/`. Editing the HTML by hand would be lost
on the next build, so every fix went into the draft, the build, the JS/CSS or the
figure scripts.

**Pipeline.**
`src/fetch_flow.py` (live API) feeds `data/processed/`, which is mutable.
`src/publication_freeze.py` freezes it into `data/publication_freezes/<date>/`, with a
hash manifest. `src/build_page.py` (standard library only) builds from the freeze.
`src/check_article.py` re-checks the freeze hashes, the embedded chart data, the
headline totals and the PNG asset hashes. Static figures come from `src/article_figs.py`
(A–D), `consent_accounting.py` (E), `jalisco_divergence.py` (F), `two_modes.py` (G),
`version_audit.py` (H), `incidence_vs_homicide.py` (I) and `outcome_vs_homicide.py` (J).

**Raw data.** The record-level mirror (23 MB), the API JSON, the CNB oficio PDF and
the parquet are gitignored. They live only in the main checkout. All 29 freeze
hashes, raw sources included, verified against the manifest on 2026-10-12.

**Deployment.** `.github/workflows/pages.yml` is an inert `workflow_dispatch` GitHub
Pages job. It deploys the committed `reports/article.html` without rebuilding it and
greps for three tokens. The repository is **private** and has **no LICENSE**.

**Existing validation.** `check_article.py`, `check_figures.py` (figure numbering),
`stage2_validate.py` (Stage-2 schema), and 15 automated claim checks recorded in the
freeze. Nothing checked the prose's derived numbers (shares, differences,
correlations, rates).

## Phase 1 — How the claims were checked

- **Independent recomputation from raw sources**, not from the project's own derived
  tables. Scratch scripts parsed the API JSON series and the 133,566-row mirror
  directly. Every headline and derived figure in the registry was recomputed. The
  freeze's derived tables match the raw sources exactly: `flow_by_year` equals the
  raw series, and `state_rates` counts equal the mirror groupby.
- **Primary documents.** CNB oficio 1349/2020 was text-extracted and the consent
  figures checked on PDF page 11. The text of Art. 108 was checked in the same document.
- **External journalism**, re-fetched on 2026-10-12: ZonaDocs 2024-11-21; A dónde van
  2021-04-08; Quinto Elemento 2024-03-06; Data Cívica 2026 (three pages); ZonaDocs/
  Lorusso 2026-04-13; news reports on the 2025 killings.
- **Outcome:** of 59 registry rows, 38 supported, 9 contradicted, 9 ambiguous, 1
  unverifiable, and 2 "supported per project sourcing, not re-fetched". Every
  contradicted or ambiguous row has a logged correction.

**What was already correct.** All 15 freeze claim checks. The headline totals and
the Figure A sums. The redaction shares and state rankings. The per-capita rates. The
homicide percentages and both r values (as arithmetic). The 2019–2024 differences (as
arithmetic). The version-audit panel figures. The consent accounting. The ZonaDocs
counts. The CDMX August figure and both drift percentages by state. The two-vintage
architecture (aggregate vs record-level), which is carefully separated throughout.

### The ten priority questions from the brief

1. **407,253 and outcome categories.** Reproduced from raw JSON; alive + dead +
   missing = total for every year. *Located* = `localizadas` = found alive + found
   dead. *Still missing* = `desaparecidas` + `no localizadas` (126,630 + 7,638).
2. **August 2026 aggregate vs December 2025 record-level.** Labelled correctly
   everywhere. The one error was the drift statement (H-4).
3. **36.8% denominator.** 49,136 / 133,566: every unresolved record in the public
   record-level version, including the 2,964 with no state. State shares use each
   state's own unresolved records. The Fig C JS "national average" was wrong (H-5).
4. **Rankings and rates.** Reproduced exactly. The national rate (98) excludes the
   no-state records; that is now stated.
5. **Homicide comparison.** Fig D compares a stock spanning decades with 2015–Oct 2025
   cumulative homicide victims. This is now stated as a period mismatch. Fig I/J
   compare year-of-disappearance cohorts (as registered by 2026) with
   year-of-investigation homicides. Different time bases, now stated.
6. **2019–2024 outcome change.** Arithmetic correct; inference wrong (B-1).
7. **Definitions.** These are current registry statuses. "Located" is an
   administrative status, and the article now ties it explicitly to the 2023 censo and
   the 2026 reclassification. No sentence treats a status change as evidence that
   someone was physically found.
8. **Disappearance year vs reporting year vs status-change year.** Fig A and the intake
   analysis use disappearance year. The public data contain no status-change dates;
   the article says so. The intake wording no longer implies filing-year dynamics (C-29).
9. **Consent documents and transparency rulings.** Numbers verified. Two
   paraphrases corrected (C-14, C-15) and one percentage reframed (C-16).
10. **Deleted, reclassified or wrongly-located cases.** "Batch deletions" became
    "batch removals" (C-23). The Quinto Elemento arithmetic was fixed (C-19). The
    unsourced Alcalde clause was removed (C-20).

## Phase 2 — Evidence published after the 15 August 2026 freeze

### Data Cívica, *A quienes nos faltan: sin datos para encontrarles* (5th edition)

Covered from 1 September 2026; consulted 2026-10-12. Coverage dates it to
**September**, not August.

| | this article | Data Cívica 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Dataset | Public record-level version, mirrored by lapanquecita | Record-level extract of the public consultation, taken February 2026, received April 2026 ("versión filtrada") |
| Date | 2 December 2025 | February 2026 |
| Confidential share | **36.8%** = 49,136 / 133,566 unresolved records | **38.5%**, denominator not stated in the published pages |
| Highest states | Jalisco 66.9, Nayarit 66.2, Yucatán 62.0, Guanajuato 59.8, Puebla 59.3 | Nayarit 71, Jalisco 66.2, Yucatán 65.3, Puebla 63.3, Guanajuato 61.8 |
| Lowest states | Chihuahua 8.8, Colima 9.0, Guerrero 9.1 | Chihuahua 11.1, Guerrero 12.1, Colima 12.5, Michoacán 17.7 |
| By year | Not observable (confidential rows lose all dates) | 17–19% for 2010–14 cohorts; over 40% for 2020–24; more than half in 2021–23; 37.1% in 2025 |

**What corroborates the article.** The order of magnitude, an almost identical state
ranking, the concentration by agency (its finding that the Tamaulipas fiscalía holds
12% of all confidential records fits §6b's Tamaulipas batch), the 70% blank reporter
contact, and registry changes made without a public log.

**What challenges it or adds caveats.**
1. Confidentiality is concentrated in recent cohorts. Because the article's intake
   analysis (§2) uses public-field records only, it excludes more than half of the
   2021–23 cohorts. A caveat has been added.
2. Falls in the still-missing count for Jalisco and Tabasco (between re-extractions a
   year apart) were roughly matched by rises in "located". Data Cívica can't confirm
   they're the same records. This argues against reading such falls as deletions, and
   for reading them as status reassignment, which feeds §6b's "located is asserted"
   claim. Caveat added.
3. 38.5% vs 36.8% is not a trend. The vintages differ, and the denominator is
   unknown. The two are cited side by side and never combined.

**Government classification (27 March 2026, pre-freeze but absent from the draft).**
132,534 missing were split into 46,742 "insufficient data", 40,308 "with
administrative activity" (5,338 locations confirmed by cross-check) and 43,128
"without activity". Data Cívica's analysis finds fewer than 2% of records lack the
fields needed to identify a person. That challenges the government's 46,742 figure.
Now in §6b, sourced to the official presentation as reported by ZonaDocs. The primary
government document was not retrieved (CNN and La Jornada returned 451/403), so it's
cited through a secondary source.

No new record-level public extract has appeared since 2 December 2025. The mirror is
still frozen, so the article's record-level vintage is still the latest available
from that source.

**Strongest unused finding: confidentiality by filing authority.** In the same state,
a record's chance of being confidential depends heavily on which authority filed it.
In Estado de México it's 3% for the fiscalía and 36% for the search commission. In
Nuevo León it's 32% and 91%. In Jalisco, 64% and 84%. Meanwhile, families' own reports
through the CNB portal are confidential at a similar rate in most states (interquartile
range 35–41%). If confidentiality reflected family choice, the reporting channel
shouldn't matter this much. It is the most direct evidence in the repository for the
article's central claim. **Added at the author's request (E-3, 2026-10-12): §6 paragraph
and Fig K,** with state offices separated from national and federal ones, and the
generalisation qualified. The gap is 30+ points in 6 of 17 states and under 10 in 8.
